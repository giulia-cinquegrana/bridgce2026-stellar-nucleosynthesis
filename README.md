## Lectures

We've just looked at the nuclear physics: reaction rates, nuclear masses, decay, and the experimental constraints on the reactions that make the elements. Now we put that physics into stars, and look at how stellar structure and evolution determine **which reactions occur, where they occur, and what gets ejected**. Tomorrow, we'll take those stellar yields and ask what happens when we put many stars together: stellar populations and, ultimately, galactic chemical evolution.

So, **why should you care?**

* **For galaxy/population people:** understand the assumptions behind your star particles, and when stellar physics becomes the bottleneck.
* **For stellar people:** understand how the physics you're modelling feeds into observable stellar populations and chemical evolution.
* **For everyone:** understand where the uncertainties in stellar yields actually come from.

### Yields: what they are and how they're calculated

We'll define what we mean by a stellar yield, how yields are calculated from stellar evolution and nucleosynthesis models, and what assumptions go into turning the composition of a model star into the material it returns to its surroundings. We’ll also look at the different ways yields can be reported and why yields from different stellar models are not always directly comparable.

- Slides: X. 

### AGB nucleosynthesis: from light elements to the s- and i-processes

We'll look at how nucleosynthesis proceeds in AGB stars, from the production of light elements through thermal pulses and dredge-up to the s-process and i-process. We’ll connect the nuclear reactions to the underlying stellar structure and evolution, and see how these processes shape the yields returned by AGB stars.
